You made a Google Business Profile. Google says it needs to be verified. Maybe it asked for a video. Maybe it said “under review” and then nothing happened for a week.

Verification is Google checking two things: that the business is real, and that you’re the one allowed to run it. Until it passes, your edits may not show, and the profile may not show up properly on Maps at all. So it’s worth doing right the first time.

## Google picks the method, not you

Depending on your business type, your area, and the profile’s history, Google may offer:

- **Video recording.** The most common one now. You film a short clip and upload it.
- **Phone or text.** A code sent to the business number.
- **Email.** A code sent to an address on your business’s domain.
- **Live video call.** You show the same things as the recording, to a Google support person.
- **Postcard.** Less common now, but still used in some places.

If you only see one option, that’s the one you have. Starting over or making a new profile to get a different option usually makes things worse, not better.

## What to film for video verification

The video has one job: show that this business operates where you say it does, and that you have access to it. Google’s checklist boils down to three things:

1. **Where you are.** Start outside. Show the street sign, a nearby business, or the building number, then your signage or entrance. If you’re a service-area business with no storefront, show your branded vehicle with its plate area, or the area you work from.
2. **What you do.** Show the equipment, stock, or workspace that proves the business: the chairs in a salon, the tools in a shop, the ovens in a kitchen.
3. **That you’re in charge.** Unlock the door, open the register, sign in to the point-of-sale system, or show a business license or utility bill with the business name. Cover anything sensitive, like account numbers.

Keep it to one continuous take. Don’t edit it, don’t add music, and don’t film your face. Walk slowly. If you’re a home-based business, film the workspace and the proof of operation, not your kitchen.

## Why verification gets rejected

The usual reasons, roughly in order:

- **The name doesn’t match what’s on the video.** The profile says “Best Plumber Toronto — 24/7 Emergency” but the van says “Ridgeway Plumbing.” Use the real name.
- **No sign of the business at that address.** A mailbox, a co-working desk, or a virtual office will fail. Google wants a place where customers come, or where the work really happens.
- **The category doesn’t fit what’s shown.** If you list “Dentist” and the video shows an office with no dental equipment, it reads as a mismatch.
- **The video is cut, shaky, or too short.** Reviewers can’t confirm what they can’t see.

## “Under review” for a while

After you submit, the profile often sits in review for a few business days, and sometimes longer. During that time:

- **Don’t keep editing.** Every change to the name, address, or category can restart the check.
- **Don’t submit a second video right away** unless Google asks for one.
- **Don’t create a new profile.** Two profiles for one business is one of the fastest ways to get both suspended.

If it’s been a couple of weeks with no answer, contact Google Business Profile support from the help center and ask for a status update. Keep a screenshot of the submission date.

## After you’re verified

Verification isn’t permanent. Big changes, like a new name, a new address, or a switch from storefront to service area, can trigger it again. Make those changes one at a time, and only when they’re true.

Once you’re in, add a second owner or manager you trust. That could be a partner or an office manager. That way the business never depends on one person’s Google account.

## What I would do this week

- Check whether your profile says “Verified” or still asks you to finish.
- Fix the business name so it matches your signage exactly.
- Plan the video before you film: outside, then the work, then the proof you run it.
- Film one clean take on a phone, in daylight, and upload it.
- Leave the profile alone while it’s under review.
