You open your Google Business Profile and see a red banner: suspended. Or you search for your business and it’s simply gone from Maps. Calls slow down, and you can’t tell why.

A suspension feels like a punishment, but it’s usually Google’s system deciding something on the profile doesn’t add up. Most suspensions can be reversed if you fix the cause and appeal with the right proof. The worst move is panicking and making a new profile.

## Two kinds of suspension

- **You can’t manage the profile, but it still shows on Maps.** This is sometimes called a soft suspension. Customers can still find you, but you can’t edit, reply to reviews, or post. Google thinks something about your ownership or the listing needs checking.
- **The profile is gone from Maps and Search.** This is the serious one. Google has decided the listing breaks its guidelines, or the business may not be real at that address. Reviews and photos stop showing while it’s down.

Either way, the fix starts the same: find the reason, correct it, then appeal.

## The usual reasons

Google rarely tells you exactly what triggered it. These cover most cases:

- **Extra words in the business name.** “Ridgeway Plumbing” is fine. “Ridgeway Plumbing — Best Emergency Plumber Toronto” is not. The name has to match your real-world signage and paperwork.
- **An address you don’t really work from.** Virtual offices, co-working desks you don’t use, PO boxes, and UPS store mailboxes are common triggers.
- **A service-area business showing its address.** If customers don’t come to you, hide the address and set service areas instead.
- **More than one profile for the same business.** Including an old one an ex-employee or agency made.
- **A category that doesn’t fit.** Or a long list of categories for services you only sometimes do.
- **A burst of edits.** Changing the name, address, phone, website, and category all in one sitting looks like someone hijacking the profile.
- **Suspicious reviews.** A wave of fake reviews can drag the whole profile into review.

## Before you appeal: fix the profile

An appeal is reviewed against what the profile shows. If the cause is still there, the appeal fails. So first:

- Change the name to exactly what’s on your sign and registration.
- Remove an address you don’t operate from, or switch to a service-area setup if that’s what you really are.
- Trim categories to the ones that truly describe the business, with the right one as primary.
- If there are duplicates, note their links so you can mention them.

Don’t keep editing after that. Make the fixes once, then appeal.

## How to appeal

Google handles this through the **Business Profile appeals tool**, which you can reach from the suspension notice or the Google Business Profile help center. You’ll be asked for proof. Good evidence includes:

- **A business license or registration** with the same name and address.
- **A utility bill or lease** for the location, in the business name.
- **Photos of your storefront signage** that include the street number, plus photos of the inside.
- **For service-area businesses:** a branded vehicle, tools of the trade, and your business registration.

Upload clear photos, not screenshots of screenshots. Make sure the name on every document matches the name on the profile. If the documents say “Ridgeway Plumbing Ltd.” and the profile says something else, fix that first.

Then wait. Reviews of appeals can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Submit once. Multiple appeals for the same profile don’t speed things up.

## What not to do

- **Don’t make a new profile.** It splits your history, loses your reviews, and a duplicate can get suspended too.
- **Don’t hire anyone who promises a guaranteed reinstatement.** No one outside Google can promise that.
- **Don’t fake documents.** Google checks, and it ends the conversation for good.

## If the appeal is denied

Read the reason, if Google gives one. Often it points to the thing still wrong: the name, the address, or the proof. Fix it, collect stronger evidence, and contact Business Profile support rather than appealing blindly again. If your business truly doesn’t meet the rules, like a home office customers never visit shown as a storefront, change the setup to match reality.

## What I would do this week

- Screenshot the suspension notice and the profile as it is now.
- Compare the profile’s name, address, and category against your signage and registration.
- Fix any mismatch in one sitting, then stop editing.
- Gather a license, a utility bill, and storefront photos with the business name on them.
- Submit one appeal and set a reminder to check it in a week.

Suspensions are stressful because the phone goes quiet. But most come down to the profile not matching the real business, and that you can fix.
