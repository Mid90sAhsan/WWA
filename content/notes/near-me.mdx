Type “plumber near me” or “dentist in [city]” and Google often shows a map and a short list of businesses before the regular blue links. That block is the map pack. For a lot of local jobs, it is the whole first screen — especially on a phone.

If you are not in it, the organic result for your website may never get seen. So it helps to know what that pack is choosing.

## It is not the same as “ranking on Google”

Website SEO and the map pack share some signals, but they are not one race. A page can rank in the blue links and still miss the map. A complete Google profile can land in the pack even when the website is thin. You want both. They do different jobs: the pack gets the tap; the site has to load and convince them to call.

## Google is asking three questions

Google has said local results lean on three ideas. In owner language:

- **Relevance.** Do you actually do the thing they typed? Categories, description, reviews that mention the work, and pages on your site that say the same thing — that’s relevance.
- **Distance.** How close are you to the searcher, or to the place they named? You cannot fake this. A shop across the street has a head start. A service-area business is judged from the area you set, not from a fake downtown suite.
- **Prominence.** How established do you look? Reviews, mentions on other real sites, a site people can use, photos, history. Prominence is slow. It is also why buying shortcuts usually backfires.

You control relevance and prominence. You do not control where the customer is standing.

## What “near me” is really doing

“Near me” means: use my location. The pack changes if you search from the shop, from home, or from the next town. That is why you and a friend get different lists. When you check your own ranking, search the way a customer would — the service plus the city — or ask someone across town to look. Don’t only search your brand name from the store Wi‑Fi.

## What actually helps you get in

No honest person can promise the #1 map spot. What I can promise is that unfinished listings lose to finished ones nearby. The work looks like this:

- A claimed, unsuspended Google Business Profile with the real name and the right primary category.
- The pin on the door people use — or a clean service area if you don’t have a public counter.
- Hours, photos, and a website that opens on a phone and repeats the same name, address, and phone.
- Reviews from real customers, replied to like a human. Old reviews still count; silence after a complaint looks worse than the complaint.
- Pages on your site that name the services and the towns you truly serve. One honest page beats ten spun city pages.

## What does not help

Stuffing the business name with keywords. Creating a second listing. Buying reviews. Dropping a pin on a mailbox you don’t work from. Those can get the profile pulled. Then you are not “ranking lower.” You are gone.

## How to read your own results without going crazy

Check once a week, from a couple of places, for the two or three phrases customers actually say. Write down who appears. If a closed business is in the pack, that’s a Google data problem — suggest an edit. If a competitor with a thinner site beats you from the same block, look at their category, photos, and reviews before you rebuild your whole site.
