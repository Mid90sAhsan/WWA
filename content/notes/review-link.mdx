Most happy customers would leave you a review. They just don’t think of it, or they don’t know where to tap. Getting more Google reviews is mostly about asking more often, at the right moment, with a link that takes them straight to the box.

## Get your Google review link

Google gives every verified profile a short link that opens the review form directly.

1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your profile.
2. Search your business name on Google, or open the Google Business Profile app.
3. Find **Ask for reviews** (sometimes under **Read reviews** or **Get more reviews**).
4. Copy the link. It looks like `g.page/r/…`.

Save it somewhere you’ll use it: your phone’s text shortcuts, your invoice template, your email signature, and a QR code at the counter. If you can’t find the option, the profile may not be verified yet. Start with [getting your Google profile verified](/notes/verification/).

## When to ask

Ask when the customer is happiest, not when you remember. For most local businesses that’s:

- **Right after the job is done,** while they’re still looking at the result. A short text that same day beats an email two weeks later.
- **When they thank you.** “Thanks, that means a lot. Would you mind saying that on Google? Here’s the link.”
- **At pickup or checkout,** with a card or QR code in hand, so they don’t have to search.

One gentle reminder a few days later is fine. More than that starts to feel like pressure.

## What to say

Keep it short and human. Something like:

> Hi Maria, thanks again for having us out today. If you have a minute, a Google review really helps a small business like ours: [link]. Even one line is great.

Telling people they can keep it short helps. “Even one line” gets more reviews than “tell us about your experience.” If you want reviews that help people find you, you can ask them to mention the service, like “if you mention the water heater, that helps others find us.” Don’t write the review for them.

## The rules that keep reviews from vanishing

Google removes reviews that break its rules, sometimes months later. Stay on the right side:

- **Ask everyone, not just happy customers.** Sending only satisfied customers to Google and steering unhappy ones elsewhere is called review gating, and Google doesn’t allow it.
- **Don’t pay or reward.** No discounts, entries, or gifts for a review, even a “fair, honest” one.
- **No reviews from staff, family, or yourself.** Google can often connect them.
- **Spread requests out.** Twenty reviews in one afternoon after months of none looks like a campaign, and filters notice. Ask steadily as jobs happen.

If reviews have already gone missing, read [why your Google reviews disappeared](/notes/reviews-disappeared/).

## How to reply, especially to the bad ones

Reply to every review. It shows you’re active, and people deciding whether to call you read the replies.

- **Good review:** thank them by name and mention the job. One or two sentences.
- **Bad review:** stay calm, keep it short, and take it offline. “I’m sorry this happened, Dan. That’s not the experience we want for anyone. Please call me at the shop so I can make it right.” Don’t argue details in public, and don’t share anything private about the customer.
- **Fake or abusive review:** don’t fire back. Reply briefly and politely, then report it through your profile’s review management. Google removes reviews that break its policies, but not ones you simply disagree with.

A calm reply to a one-star review often does more for you than five more five-star reviews. Future customers want to see how you handle problems.

## What I would do this week

- Copy your review link and save it as a text shortcut on your phone.
- Add it to your invoice or receipt template.
- Print a small QR card for the counter or the truck.
- Text the last ten customers you know were happy.
- Reply to every review you haven’t answered yet, oldest first.

Reviews add up slowly, and that’s fine. A steady few a month from real customers is what Google trusts and what people believe.
