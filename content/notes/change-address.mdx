You’re moving the shop, or the address on Google was wrong from the start. Either way, you want the new address showing on Maps without losing the reviews you worked for.

The key is simple: **update the profile you already have.** Don’t create a new one. Your reviews, photos, and history belong to the profile, and they move with it.

## How to change the address

1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your profile.
2. Search your business name on Google, or open the Google Business Profile app.
3. Choose **Edit profile**, then **Location** (sometimes **Business address**).
4. Enter the new address and check where the pin lands. Drag it to the door customers use if it’s off.
5. Save.

Google usually reviews address changes before they go live. That can take anywhere from a few minutes to several days. In some cases Google asks you to verify again, often with a short video at the new location. If that happens, see [getting your Google profile verified](/notes/verification/).

## Time it with the actual move

Change the address when customers can actually come to the new place, not weeks before. If Google checks the new address and finds an empty unit with no sign, the change may be rejected or the profile flagged.

A good order:

1. Get your signage up at the new location, even a temporary one.
2. Update the address on Google the day you open there.
3. Update your website the same day.
4. Then update everywhere else, over the next week or two.

## What happens to your reviews and ranking

- **Reviews stay.** They’re attached to the profile, not the address.
- **Your Maps position will change.** Distance is a big part of who shows up in local search, so moving across town means competing with a new set of neighbors. Expect some ups and downs for a few weeks.
- **Old directions will update.** Customers who saved your old location may still get sent there for a little while. A sign on the old door helps.

## If you’re a service-area business

If customers don’t come to you, your address should be hidden anyway. Moving your home base still matters, because Google uses it behind the scenes. Update it, and adjust your service areas if the move changes where you actually work. Don’t pick service areas far beyond where you really go.

## Mistakes that cause trouble

- **Creating a new profile for the new location.** If you’re closing the old place and opening a new one, it’s the same business. A second profile splits your reviews and can get both suspended.
- **Marking the old profile “permanently closed” and starting fresh.** You lose everything you built. Only do this if it’s truly a different business.
- **Changing everything at once.** A new address, new name, new phone number, and new category in one sitting looks suspicious. Change the address first. Make other changes later, one at a time, if they’re needed.
- **Using a virtual office or mailbox as the new address.** It won’t pass, and it can get the profile suspended. See [what to do when your Google profile is suspended](/notes/suspended/).

## Update the address everywhere else

Google compares your profile with what the rest of the web says about you. After the move, update:

- Your website: the footer, the contact page, and any map embed.
- Apple Maps (through Apple Business Connect) and Bing Places.
- Yelp, Facebook, and any industry directories you’re listed on.
- Your local chamber of commerce or business association listing.
- Your invoices, email signature, and quote templates.

You don’t need to chase every small directory. Get the big ones right and match the name, address, and phone exactly.

## What I would do this week

- Confirm you can sign in to the profile and that it’s verified.
- Put up signage at the new address before you update anything.
- Change the address on Google on opening day and check the pin.
- Update your website the same day.
- Make a short list of other listings and work through it over the next two weeks.
