Yesterday you had 38 reviews. Today you have 31. Or a customer swears they left you a five-star review, and you can’t find it anywhere.

This happens to a lot of businesses, and it usually isn’t personal. Google removes or hides reviews for a handful of reasons. Some come back on their own. Some don’t. Here’s how to tell which is which.

## First: check whether they’re really gone

Before you do anything, look carefully:

- **Search from a different device or a private window.** Review counts sometimes differ for a few hours or days as Google updates.
- **Check the reviews section inside your profile,** not just the star count on Maps. The count can lag behind.
- **Ask the customer to look at their own Google Maps profile** under Contributions. If they can see their review but you can’t, it was probably filtered and is only visible to them.

If the numbers come back within a day or two, it was a display glitch. If not, keep reading.

## The usual reasons reviews disappear

**1. Google’s spam filter caught them.** This is the most common one. Google checks every review automatically, and sometimes long after it was posted. Reviews can get filtered if many arrive at once, if they come from the same Wi-Fi network (like customers leaving reviews on a tablet at your counter), if the reviewer’s account is brand new, or if a link or phone number appears in the text. Real reviews get caught too.

**2. The review broke a rule.** Reviews that were rewarded with a discount, written by staff or family, collected only from happy customers, or full of off-topic or offensive content can be removed. This applies even if you didn’t know the rule at the time. See [how to ask for Google reviews](/notes/review-link/) for the safe way to do it.

**3. The reviewer deleted it or their account.** If someone removes their Google account, or Google removes it for spam, every review from that account goes with it. You won’t be told.

**4. Something changed on your profile.** Merging duplicate listings, changing the business name or address in a big way, or moving the profile to a new category can shuffle reviews. Usually they reappear. Sometimes reviews tied to an old duplicate don’t carry over.

**5. Your profile was suspended or restricted.** While a profile is suspended, its reviews may not show. If you can’t edit your profile or it has a warning on it, start with [what to do when your Google Business Profile is suspended](/notes/suspended/).

**6. Google found fake reviews on your profile.** If Google decides a profile has received fake reviews, it can remove them in bulk. It may also pause new reviews for a while and show a notice to customers. This can happen even if a competitor or an old marketing company left them. Stop any review service you’re paying for, right away.

## What you can do

- **Appeal reviews Google removed.** In your profile’s review management, Google lists some removed reviews and lets you appeal the decision. Appeal only the ones you know came from real customers.
- **Report the problem to support** if a large chunk vanished at once and none of the reasons above fit. Include the dates and a rough count.
- **Ask the customer to post again, later, from their own phone.** Not at your counter, not on your Wi-Fi. Don’t ask them to repost straight away. Wait a week or two.
- **Keep getting reviews the normal way.** Steady, real reviews over time are the best fix for losing a few.

## What not to do

- **Don’t buy reviews to make up the number.** That’s often how the bulk removals start.
- **Don’t create a new profile** to get a clean slate. Duplicate profiles get suspended, and you’ll lose the reviews you still have.
- **Don’t bombard Google support** with the same request every day. One clear message with the details does more.

## What I would do this week

- Screenshot your review count and the reviews you can see, so you have a record.
- Check whether any of your requests went out in a big batch or from your shop’s Wi-Fi.
- Appeal any removed reviews you know were real.
- Move your review requests to a text sent after the job, with your review link.
- Reply to the reviews that remain, so the profile looks active.
