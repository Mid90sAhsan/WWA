## 1. Do I need a website if I have a Google Business Profile?

- **Slug:** `website-vs-profile`
- **Card title:** Do you need a website if you’re on Google?
- **Searcher’s question:** “I already show up on Maps. Why pay for a site?”

**Sections**
- **The short answer:** the profile gets you found, and the website gets you chosen. Most people check both before they call.
- **What a profile can’t do:** no room to explain services, prices, service areas, or why you’re different. You don’t control the layout, and competitors’ ads appear on it.
- **What Google uses the site for:** it confirms the name, address, and phone (link to the maps note, reason 5). The site is also where the profile’s “Website” button goes, and a broken link loses the visitor.
- **When a profile alone is fine:** brand-new businesses, or jobs taken only by phone in a tiny town. Be honest about this.
- **What the site has to have:** one page per main service, the service area, a phone number you can tap, real photos, and fast loading on a phone.
- **What I would do this week:** a checklist.
- **Links:** maps note, map pack note, audit.

## 2. How long does it take to show up on Google Maps?

- **Slug:** `how-long-maps`
- **Card title:** How long until you show up on Maps?
- **Searcher’s question:** “I verified last week. Why can’t I find myself?”

**Sections**
- **Verification is not the same as visibility:** you can have a live profile that still sits far down the list.
- **Rough timelines, with honest ranges:** a new profile going live takes days. Edits being accepted take days to a couple of weeks. Showing up for competitive searches can take months, or may never happen from far away.
- **Why distance caps it:** the map pack note covers the three questions Google asks. You can’t make yourself closer.
- **Things that reset the clock:** changing the name, address, or category, and suspensions.
- **How to check without fooling yourself:** search from a phone while standing near the business, use a private window, and ask a customer to search for you.
- **What I would do this week:** a checklist.

## 3. Service-area business vs. storefront: which should you be on Google?

- **Slug:** `service-area-vs-storefront`
- **Card title:** Storefront or service area?
- **Searcher’s question:** plumbers, cleaners, mobile groomers, and contractors working from home.

**Sections**
- **What the two settings mean:** whether your address shows or stays hidden.
- **Google’s rule in plain words:** show the address only if customers come to you at that place, during the hours you list.
- **The common mistakes:** listing a home address, using a virtual office or UPS box, or drawing a service area across the whole state.
- **What the setting changes:** what customers see, and what it doesn’t change. A hidden address still counts for distance.
- **How to pick service areas:** cities and regions you actually serve, and not too many.
- **On the website:** a page or section for each area you really cover. No thin “plumber in [city]” pages copied 40 times.
- **Risk callout:** a wrong setting is a common cause of suspension. Link to the maps note, reason 2.

## 4. Why did my Google reviews disappear?

- **Slug:** `reviews-disappeared`
- **Card title:** Where did your reviews go?
- **Searcher’s question:** “I had 38 reviews yesterday and 31 today.”

**Sections**
- **First, don’t panic:** reviews sometimes vanish for a while, then come back.
- **The usual reasons:** Google’s spam filter catches reviews sent from one place or posted in a burst, review-gating or incentives, the reviewer deleted their account, a profile merge or duplicate, or a suspension.
- **What you can and can’t do:** appeal through Google’s review management tool. You can’t get filtered reviews restored on demand.
- **How to ask for reviews so they stick:** ask everyone, not just happy customers, spread requests out over time, send a direct link, and never pay or offer discounts.
- **Replying to reviews:** short and specific. Reply to bad reviews calmly, because future customers read the reply more than the review.
- **What I would do this week:** a checklist.

## 5. What a “free SEO audit” email is really offering

- **Slug:** `seo-spam-emails`
- **Card title:** That “your site has 47 errors” email
- **Searcher’s question:** owners who get cold emails and calls about ranking #1.

**Sections**
- **What these emails usually are:** automated tool output sent to thousands of businesses at once.
- **Red flags:** guaranteed rankings, “Google partner” claims that mean nothing, asking to own your profile, long contracts, and secret methods.
- **What a real review looks like:** it’s specific to your business and city, explains why each item matters, and lists things you could fix yourself.
- **Who should own what:** you should always be the owner of your Google profile, domain, and website login. Add helpers as managers.
- **How to vet anyone, me included:** ask for the logins to stay in your name, ask what they’ll do in the first 30 days, and ask how they’ll report results.
- **The ending:** an honest mention of the free audit, framed as “here’s what mine includes and what it doesn’t.”
- **Note:** this builds trust and sets the site apart. Keep the tone fair, not bitter.
